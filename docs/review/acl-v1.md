## Claims to confirm

### Anatomy and function

1. The ACL is intracapsular but extrasynovial.
2. It runs from the posteromedial aspect of the lateral femoral condyle to the
   anterior intercondylar area of the tibia.
3. It has two functional bundles, anteromedial and posterolateral, named for
   their tibial insertions.
4. The anteromedial bundle tightens in flexion and the posterolateral bundle
   tightens closer to extension.
5. It is the primary restraint to anterior tibial translation, taking "the large
   majority" of the restraining force. **The page avoids a percentage on
   purpose. If you want a figure here, please give the one you would stand
   behind.**
6. It acts as a secondary restraint to internal rotation and to varus and valgus
   angulation once the collaterals are compromised.
7. Blood supply is mainly from the middle geniculate artery.
8. Innervation from the posterior articular branch of the tibial nerve carries
   mechanoreceptors, and this underlies the proprioceptive deficit after rupture.

### Examination and imaging

9. Lachman is the most sensitive clinical test, performed at about twenty to
   thirty degrees of flexion.
10. The anterior drawer at ninety degrees is less sensitive, particularly acutely.
11. Pivot shift grade corresponds more closely to symptoms than translation alone.
12. A Segond fracture implies a cruciate rupture until proven otherwise.
13. A tibial spine avulsion is the skeletally immature equivalent.
14. A deep lateral femoral notch sign is an indirect indicator.
15. Lateral compartment bone bruising supports the mechanism.

### Decision making and technique

16. Reconstruction is indicated on instability and demand rather than on the scan.
17. Allograft in a young pivoting athlete carries a "materially higher" failure
    rate. **Please confirm the strength of wording.**
18. Operating on a stiff, swollen knee with poor quadriceps control increases the
    risk of arthrofibrosis.
19. A locked knee from a displaced bucket handle tear warrants earlier surgery to
    save the meniscus.
20. Lateral extra articular tenodesis reduces graft failure in selected high risk
    patients.
21. Listed failure modes: non anatomic tunnel placement, missed posterolateral
    corner injury, unaddressed ramp lesion or meniscal deficiency, excessive
    posterior tibial slope, premature return to sport.

### Revision

22. Confluent, widened or badly malpositioned tunnels need bone grafting and a
    staged approach.
23. Staging interval should follow graft incorporation rather than a fixed number
    of months.
24. Slope reducing anterior closing wedge osteotomy has a place in revision with
    markedly increased slope, threshold unsettled.
25. Revision outcomes sit below primary outcomes for return to sport and for
    patient reported scores.

### Rehabilitation

26. Extension lost early is difficult to regain later.
27. Return before criteria are met is associated with higher re rupture rates,
    particularly in adolescents.
28. The uninjured side carries a meaningful subsequent injury risk.

### Junior and patient tiers

29. Junior text: "training programmes that teach better landing and turning lower
    the risk, and they work best when a squad does them twice a week through the
    whole season rather than only in pre season." **Please check the frequency
    claim specifically.**
30. Junior text states girls and women tear the ACL more often than boys and men
    in the same sport, and that the reasons are still being studied.
31. Patient text: walking in a straight line is often comfortable early, which is
    why some tears are missed.
32. Patient red flags: cannot bear weight after injury, hot swollen knee with
    fever, new numbness or coldness in the foot.
