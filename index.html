Anterior cruciate ligament

Choose a depth

The knee's strongest brake

The ACL is a short, tough band deep inside the knee. It stops the shin bone sliding forward when you turn, land or stop quickly. An athlete who tears it often hears a pop, and the knee swells the same day. Warm up programmes that train landing and turning lower the risk. If a knee gives way, swells or hurts to stand on, tell a parent, a teacher or a coach, and have it looked at by a health professional.

What it is and why it matters

A strong cord inside the knee that stops the shin sliding forward. People often describe a pop, swelling within a few hours, and a knee that gives way on turning. Not every tear needs surgery. The decision depends on your symptoms and what you need the knee to do.

Structure and function

An intracapsular, extrasynovial ligament running from the medial wall of the lateral femoral condyle to the anterior intercondylar area of the tibia. Two functional bundles, anteromedial and posterolateral. It is the primary restraint to anterior tibial translation and a secondary restraint to rotation.

Examination and imaging

Lachman is the most sensitive clinical test. The pivot shift demonstrates rotatory instability and correlates with function. A Segond fracture on plain radiograph implies a rupture until proven otherwise. MRI confirms the tear and grades meniscal, ramp and chondral injury.

Decision and evidence

Reconstruct on instability and demand rather than on the scan alone. Graft choice trades re-rupture risk against donor site morbidity and is age dependent. Missed posterolateral corner injury, ramp lesion or excessive tibial slope are recognised causes of early graft failure.

Revision and complex cases

Revision starts with tunnel position and tunnel width on CT. Stage with bone graft when tunnels are confluent or wide. Address meniscal deficiency, coronal malalignment and slope. Add lateral extra-articular tenodesis in high grade pivot, young pivoting athletes and revision.

Open questions

Repair versus reconstruction in proximal avulsion. The slope threshold that justifies anterior closing wedge osteotomy. Whether criterion based return to sport testing changes re-rupture rates in adolescents. How registry data should shape graft choice in routine practice.

Rules for this tier

  • No commercial content. No products, no supplements and no clinical services anywhere in Section 0. Marketing to minors has no place in an educational brand.
  • No clinical advice. Every symptom page closes by pointing the reader to a parent, a teacher, a coach or a doctor. No doses, no self treatment, no rehabilitation prescriptions.
  • Privacy by design. Minimal data, no behavioural advertising, high privacy defaults, and parental consent where an account is offered.
  • Moderation first. Questions and leaderboards are moderated before they appear. There is no open chat and no direct messaging for under 18s.
  • Plain English. Chapters 0.1 and 0.2 target a reading age of 12 to 14 and pass a readability review before publication.
  • Safeguarding sign off. A named lead approves outreach and any content that features an identifiable young person.

Knee pain is rarely only the knee

A joint fails where load meets tolerance. KneeSchool teaches both sides of that equation, so the plan covers the person and not only the scan.

  • Hip and trunk control

    Weak abductors and poor trunk control shift load onto the medial compartment and the patella.

  • Foot and ankle

    Stiff ankles and collapsing arches change how force travels up the limb during landing and running.

  • Training history

    Most overuse injury follows a change in volume, surface or footwear within the preceding six weeks.

  • Sleep and stress

    Poor sleep lowers pain thresholds and slows tissue repair, which alters the response to rehabilitation.

  • Metabolic health

    Body mass, diabetes and smoking influence cartilage health, wound healing and surgical risk.

  • Previous injury

    An earlier meniscal or ligament injury remains the strongest predictor of the next one.

How a page reaches this site

Every page passes four stages in order. The last stage is a person, and there is no route around it.

Draft to a brief

A page starts from a brief that names its tiers, what it must cover and what it must leave to a deeper page. The second list is what keeps the spiral a spiral.

Verify the evidence

A separate pass treats the draft as suspect. It confirms each reference exists and that the source supports the claim as written, at the stated size and population.

Edit for plain speech

A third pass holds British spelling, short sentences and varied rhythm. It works from the verified text alone, so it cannot introduce a new fact.

Consultant review

A consultant knee surgeon approves, amends or rejects. An amendment returns to the style gate. Nothing publishes by any other route.

Rules that cannot be waived

  • Consultant sign off is mandatory. There is no path from the automated checks to publication that avoids a named reviewer.
  • No invented references. A reference that cannot be retrieved is removed. A missing reference is acceptable. A fabricated one is a failure.
  • No commercial content for minors. The junior tier carries no product placement, no supplement content and no promotion of clinical services.
  • Guidance carries a date. Every management page records the guideline version it was checked against, and that check repeats each year.
  • Uncertainty is stated. Where the evidence is thin or conflicting, the page says so under its own heading rather than settling the question by tone.

The style gate

House style is enforced by a deterministic check rather than by judgement, because an editor working at volume will eventually let something through and a rule will not.

It rejects em and en dashes, a list of stock phrases, American spellings, any citation marker without a matching reference, and any citation marker at all in junior or patient text. A page that fails goes back for another edit, twice at most, then to a human.
